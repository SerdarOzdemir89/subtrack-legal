Sowbook for iOS · Updated 5 November 2026

Sowbook plans a vegetable garden from two dates: the last frost of spring and the first frost of autumn. Everything else is counted from there.

Setting the frost dates

On first run, pick the closest region and adjust the two dates to what your own garden actually does. You can change them any time in Settings → Frost dates. The regional dates are averages, not a forecast.

Why is this week empty?

If no sowing, transplanting or harvest window falls in the current week, the app says so. The Crops tab shows every crop's windows for the season, so you can see what is coming next.

How succession sowing works

For crops that are sown in rows (lettuce, radish, carrots, beans and others), Sowbook suggests a new sowing every two to three weeks until there is still enough time to mature before the first frost. Each suggestion appears in This week when its date arrives.

Beds and squares

One grid square is one square foot. Tap an empty square to plant, tap a plant to open it. Tall crops need more than one square; the crop detail page shows the spacing and how many plants share a square.

Rotation warning

When you plant a crop in a bed that grew the same family last season, the app warns you. It is advice, not a block; you can still plant.

Restoring a purchase

Open Settings → Sowbook Pro → Restore purchase. Purchases are tied to your Apple account. If nothing restores, check that you are signed in with the Apple account that bought Pro.

Exporting

Settings → Export CSV or Export PDF creates a file you can save or share. The PDF is an A4 plan with each bed and its plantings; the CSV opens in Numbers or Excel.

Deleting data

Settings → Delete all data removes every garden, bed, planting, harvest and seed lot from the device. There is no server copy to delete.
